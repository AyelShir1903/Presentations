Why this matters here

In a regulated function, the cost of a miss is not the email you read late. It is the one you never saw.

A typical Monday for this team: regulator correspondence, redlines from outside counsel, business teams asking for a view, internal audit follow-ups, board deadlines, and the newsletters and vendor mail that sit on top of all of it.

  • Requests for an opinion arrive as "quick question" and look like noise.
  • Deadlines live inside attachments and three-week-old threads.
  • Regulator and audit mail competes for attention with everything else at 8:30 on Monday.

What a miss looks like

  • A regulator response deadline passed because the request sat in a cc'd thread
  • A sanctions question answered by Treasury without Legal, because it was titled "quick question"
  • Board papers submitted late because the deadline was in a PDF nobody opened

What Copilot changes

It does not reduce the volume. It changes where you look first and how fast you understand what you are looking at.

Job 1 · The prioritization list

Eight starting rules for this team, ready to paste into Settings

Treat as high priority
It's from a regulator or supervisory authority
It's from external counsel or a law firm
It asks for a legal opinion, advice or sign-off
It mentions a deadline, due date or response date
It's about an information request, investigation, subpoena or enforcement
It mentions sanctions, bribery, whistleblowing or a data breach
Mark as low priority
It's a newsletter or legal news digest
It's a vendor webinar, event or marketing email

Tune after one week

  • Filter to High and read the reasons: which rule fired, and was it right?
  • Add a rule for any sender or matter you had to hunt for
  • Delete a rule that keeps firing on noise; "mentions a deadline" is the usual suspect

Wording note

Name your real regulators, law firms and systems in the rules ("It's an automatic notification from [matter system]"). Specific phrases rank better than general ones.

Job 1 · Limits

Copilot ranks by signals, not by legal risk. Plant one it gets wrong and let the room find it.

Quick question

Dana Okafor, Treasury · Mon 08:41 · to you
✦ Normal priority
Internal message, no deadline stated, short request from a colleague.
Hi, can we settle the Astoria Holdings invoice through their new entity in Dubai instead? Finance wants to release by 3pm. Thanks!
What the lawyer sees: a change of counterparty to a new entity in a different jurisdiction, same day, no screening mentioned. This is a sanctions and KYC question, and Copilot ranked it level with the HR policy refresh.

What Prioritize does not do

  • Rank mail that arrived before you switched it on; changing rules later does not re-rank old mail
  • Look outside the main Inbox: rules that move mail to subfolders bypass it; shared mailboxes are not evaluated
  • Evaluate meeting mail, out-of-office replies, mail the sender marked low importance, mail to yourself, encrypted mail it cannot read
  • Replace Focused/Other; it adds a marker on top

Availability

Needs a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence, is rolling out gradually, and supports major languages first. Check it works in your tenant the week before.

Rules for this group

Three things to know before you trust it with privileged material

1

It stays inside the boundary

Copilot in Outlook works on mail you already have access to, inside the firm's Microsoft 365 tenant. It does not give you access to anything new, and it does not train on your mail.

2

A summary is not the document

Summaries and drafts are your work product once you use them. Read the source before you rely on a date, an obligation or a quotation. Review every draft before it leaves.

3

Prompts and answers are records

Copilot interactions are stored in your mailbox and fall under the same retention and eDiscovery policies as email. Write prompts the way you write email.

What it does not replaceLegal judgment, approvals, privilege decisions and record-keeping obligations. Copilot shortens the reading; it does not shorten the thinking.
Confirm points 1 and 3 with IT and Information Governance for your tenant's configuration before presenting11 / 14

Rules for this group · Discoverability

Your Copilot chat is a document, and documents get produced

How the record is created

1
You type a prompt in Copilot Chat; Copilot answers.
2
Both are copied into a hidden folder in your own Exchange mailbox. You cannot see the folder; the organization can.
3
Retention policies, litigation holds and eDiscovery holds apply to that folder exactly as they do to email.
4
Purview eDiscovery can search, collect and export it. "Delete" in the app moves the chat to a holds folder; it does not remove the retained copy.
What is captured varies by tenant. Microsoft documents this for Copilot Chat; confirm with IT and Information Governance which in-app actions (summaries, drafts) your tenant retains, and for how long.

Why it reaches a courtroom or a regulator

  • Discovery rules reach electronic data. In Canadian civil procedure a "document" includes data and information in electronic form, and every relevant document in a party's possession, control or power must be disclosed unless privileged (for example Ontario Rule 30). Other common-law jurisdictions have equivalents.
  • Regulators have their own powers. An information request like the one in today's demo can reach stored Copilot interactions as readily as it reaches email.
  • A hold freezes it. Once litigation or an investigation is reasonably anticipated, the retained copy is preserved whatever you do in the app.
  • Privilege is claimed, not automatic. A prompt that reflects legal advice may be privileged, but each item is reviewed on its own. Prompts that mix advice with business chatter, or that read as venting, weaken the claim.
Working ruleWrite every prompt as if opposing counsel will read it aloud. Keep privileged work in privileged channels, label it, and never paste advice into a prompt that also carries business talk.
Microsoft Learn, Learn about retention for Copilot; discovery points are general and should be confirmed with litigation counsel for your jurisdiction12 / 14

Start this week

One habit, five prompts, and a person to ask

The habit

Before you read anything on Monday, filter to High priority and run prompt 1. Ten minutes, then read as normal.

Who to ask

Name the two or three daily Copilot users in the room as go-to helpers. Pair each with a newcomer for the first two weeks.
