Applying the Privacy Residual Risk Decision Rule

How to score a privacy risk so that anyone with the same facts reaches the same rating. Click through the slides in order; each is short. The calculator in Part 3 lets you try the rule on your own numbers.

Four inputs per riskSeven calculation steps24 slides, about 30 minutes2 November 2026
Part 1 · Understand

Same facts, same rating, whoever does the assessment

Questionnaires give evidence, not ratings

An answer in a PIA questionnaire tells you what someone says is in place. The rating comes from what you can show.

The rule decides one step only

It fixes how inherent risk and controls combine into a residual rating. Scoping, risk identification and the approval table are unchanged.

Your judgment still matters

You still decide impact, likelihood, effectiveness and evidence. The rule only stops two analysts combining the same judgments differently.

Part 2 · Enter

Before you score: write the risk clearly

Fill in the four parts and watch the statement form. If you cannot fill the harm, the risk is probably not material. If one sentence holds two events, split it into two rows.

Three rules

  • One row per distinct risk
  • Split a risk when the cause, the people affected, the control or the harm differs
  • Score one gap once; if two rows share a gap, the rationale says which row carries it
PIA setting 1 of 2

The data class sets the minimum impact

Most sensitive data in scopeMinimum inherent impact
Public or effectively de-identified information1 Insignificant
Basic contact information, mostly business contact details2 Minor
Ordinary personal information, policy and non-injury claims details3 Moderate
Highly Confidential: banking, licence, SIN, health, injury claims, underwriting or credit decisions4 Major
Identity-takeover combinations, biometrics, at-risk location data, criminal or immigration status, children’s data5 Extreme

Pick the most sensitive data element anywhere in the processing. If you score a risk below the minimum, the sheet flags it. That is allowed when the harm is not exposure of that data (late deletion, an unclear notice, loss of control). Say so in the rationale, because a reviewer will see the flag.

PIA setting 2 of 2

The modifier moves the band up one step

Scale

More than 10,000 individuals affected.

Count people, not records. If the questionnaire gives records, say what you assumed.

Vulnerable group

The people affected are mostly vulnerable: minors, claimants in crisis.

“Mostly” is the test. Some vulnerable people in a general population does not qualify.

How it applies

  • Set once for the whole PIA
  • One step only, even if both apply
  • Low to Medium, Medium to High, High stays High
  • Applied at the inherent stage and again at the residual stage

Scale and vulnerability describe the processing, not the controls, so good controls do not remove the modifier. A large Highly Confidential initiative therefore stays High at residual; the controls decide the action list, not the band. This is deliberate policy, confirmed by the Privacy Director.

Input 1 of 4

Inherent impact: the worst credible harm to one person

LevelHarm to the most exposed personTypical data
1 InsignificantNo discernible harm; trivial, immediately reversible inconveniencePublic or de-identified
2 MinorLimited concern or short-lived loss of control; readily remediedBasic contact details
3 ModerateMaterial distress, financial inconvenience or loss of privacy; reversible with effortOrdinary personal information
4 MajorSerious financial, identity, physical or discriminatory harm that is difficult to reverseBanking, licence, SIN, health
5 ExtremeCatastrophic or widespread harm to safety, livelihood or fundamental rightsIdentity-takeover sets, biometrics, children

Score before controls. “Credible” means a plausible outcome given the data, the recipients and realistic misuse, not a theoretical worst case. Use the data column as an anchor, not a lookup: bank details usually mean Major, but a risk about late deletion of ordinary data is Moderate even in a Highly Confidential PIA. Impact does not change at the residual stage, so this score carries all the way through.

Input 2 of 4

Inherent likelihood: how likely without the controls

LevelMeaningSigns you would see
1 RareOnly in exceptional circumstancesInternal use, few users, few people affected
2 UnlikelyCould happen, not expectedLimited sharing, mature process
3 PossibleMight happenVendor involved, manual handling, new process
4 LikelyWill probably happenCross-border transfer, sub-processors, broad access, new use
5 Almost certainExpected to happenA known gap left open

Imagine the controls you are about to assess are not there. The commonest error is to score likelihood with the controls already in mind, which counts them twice. Score the raw exposure: who touches the data, how, how often, how new the process is. A new vendor with a cloud host and vendor support access is Likely. A long-running internal process with two users is Rare or Unlikely.

Between the inputs

Name the weakest control you are relying on

Three steps

  1. List the controls that actually reduce this risk. Ignore controls that address a different risk.
  2. Pick the weakest one. Where several work together, the weakest sets the reduction for the whole set.
  3. Choose its name from the catalogue. Put the detail (which system, which vendor) in the rationale.

The pathway map checks your choice

  • The control must be on the map for the risk category, or it earns no reduction
  • Governance controls (roles, policies, training, inventories, monitoring) support every risk but earn no reduction on their own
  • A control typed by hand, outside the catalogue, earns no reduction

The map is an eligibility list, not a checklist. A mapped control that is absent is not scored as None; its absence shows in the effectiveness of the controls you do rely on.

Between the inputs

Which controls count for which risk

Highlighted controls may earn a reduction for this risk. Dimmed ones address a different pathway. Governance controls support every risk but earn no reduction on their own.

Input 3 of 4

Control effectiveness: design and operation

Level (Risk team scale)What it looks like in practiceCan earn a reduction?
1 Fully effectiveDesigned for this pathway and operating as intended, with no known gaps or exceptionsYes, up to 2
2 Mostly effectiveOperating, with minor gaps that do not leave the pathway openYes, 1
3 Partially effectiveCovers part of the pathway, or operates inconsistently; the event stays credibleNo
4 Mostly ineffectiveExists on paper or in part; rarely works as intendedNo
5 None or ineffectiveAbsent, not yet in place, expired or not operatingNo

Two questions: is the control designed to interrupt this specific pathway, and is it operating? Rate it as it is today, not as it will be after the actions. A planned, draft or expired control is level 5. A control that works for most users but not for the vendor support role is Partially effective, because the pathway is still open for that group.

Input 4 of 4

Evidence status: what you can show

Supported

Independent audit or test result, logs or configuration evidence dated within the last 12 months, showing the control operating as designed.

Example: a SOC 2 Type II report in hand, period within 12 months, scope covers the service.

Partially supported

Design evidence only (a policy, a contract clause, settings without operating evidence), or operating evidence older than 12 months.

Example: a signed clause requiring access reviews, but no review has run yet.

Not verified

A questionnaire answer or attestation only; planned, draft or expired controls.

Example: the business lead ticks “MFA” on the PIA form and nothing else is provided.

A questionnaire answer on its own is always Not verified. Ask for the evidence; if it does not arrive, record the gap as an action and score what you have. Write the evidence reference, with its date, in the rationale column so the next reviewer can find it.

The reduction table is the heart of the rule

EffectivenessSupportedPartially supportedNot verified

How to read it

The reduction is the number of likelihood levels the control removes: 0, 1 or 2.

It is the lower of what the effectiveness earns and what the evidence earns. Strong control, weak evidence: little or no reduction.

Four of the fifteen cells earn anything. That is the point: a reduction is for controls you can show are working. Hover a cell to see its row and column.

Part 3 · Read

The matrix turns impact and likelihood into a band

Used twice

Once with inherent impact and inherent likelihood, to get the inherent band.

Once more with the same impact and the residual likelihood (after the reduction), to get the residual band.

Because impact never changes at the residual stage, a risk only ever moves left along its row as the reduction lowers likelihood. Read along the Major row: a reduction can move a risk from High to Medium, never to Low. That is the Highly Confidential floor at work.

Part 3 · Read

What the workbook calculates, in seven steps

  1. Inherent band = matrix of inherent impact and inherent likelihood.
  2. Modifier moves the inherent band up one step, if the PIA has one.
  3. Pathway check: the named control must be on the map for the risk category, or the reduction is 0.
  4. Reduction = reduction table of effectiveness and evidence: 0, 1 or 2.
  1. Residual likelihood = inherent likelihood minus the reduction, never below Rare. Impact stays as scored.
  2. Residual band = matrix of impact and residual likelihood; the modifier moves it up one step again.
  3. Cap: the residual band can never be higher than the inherent band.

PIA rating = the highest residual band across all risks. Never an average.

You never run these by hand; the grey columns do. You should still be able to follow them, because a reviewer will ask why a row came out the way it did, and the answer is always one of these seven.

    Highest, never average

    One High risk makes the PIA High. Seven Lows do not dilute it.

    Record both ratings

    The current rating decides. The expected rating after actions is reported beside it, never instead of it.

    High goes on the register

    Every High rating and every accepted High risk is recorded in the privacy risk register and the quarterly ERM report.

    Part 4 · Decide

    Six mistakes that break reproducibility

    Giving a reduction for a planned, draft, expired or self-attested control
    Score it as it is today (level 5 or Not verified); put the fix in the actions and let the expected rating show the gain.
    Scoring likelihood with the controls already in mind
    Score the raw exposure first; the controls earn their reduction in inputs 3 and 4. Otherwise they are counted twice.
    Scoring the same gap in several rows
    Name one row that carries it; the other rationales point to that row.
    Averaging the risks to get the PIA rating
    The PIA rating is the highest residual band, always.
    Lowering the impact because a control limits the harm
    Impact stays as scored; a reduction of impact is a Chief Privacy Officer exception, written in the rationale and the register.
    Typing over a calculated cell to get the band you expected
    Change an input you can defend, or raise the rationale with the Privacy Office. Grey cells are never edited: a typed-over band breaks the audit trail for every row after it.
